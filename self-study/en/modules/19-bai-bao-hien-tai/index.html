The problem: a fast Helstrom bound for turbulent FSO links

Helstrom bound for two hypotheses with equal priors
\[ P_e^{\min}=\tfrac12\Bigl(1-\bigl\|\tfrac12(\rho_1-\rho_0)\bigr\|_1\Bigr)=\tfrac12\Bigl(1-\sum_k|\lambda_k|\Bigr) \]
  • \(\rho_0,\rho_1\)received density matrices for the two bits
  • \(\lambda_k\)eigenvalues of \(\tfrac12(\rho_1-\rho_0)\)
Aim of the paper
\[ (\,|\alpha|,\sigma,L,C_n^2,\lambda\,)\ \longmapsto\ \hat P_e\approx P_e^{\min},\qquad \frac{t_{\rm SDP}}{t_{\rm surrogate}}=\frac{114}{0.018}\approx6.3\times10^{3} \]
  • \(\hat P_e\)surrogate prediction
  • \(114/0.018\)\(\approx6.3\times10^{3}\) times faster when batched
📖 Plain-language explanation for beginners
To know how often a laser link through the air errs, a quantum problem must be solved. Solving it exactly is slow; a neural network learns a shortcut but must still respect the physics.

Two coherent states and the noisy channel

BPSK coherent-state pair and overlap
\[ |\psi_0\rangle=|{+}\alpha\rangle,\ |\psi_1\rangle=|{-}\alpha\rangle,\qquad |\langle\psi_0|\psi_1\rangle|^2=e^{-4|\alpha|^2}=e^{-4\bar n} \]
  • \(\bar n=|\alpha|^2\)mean photon number
  • \(e^{-4\bar n}\)squared overlap
Received signal after the channel (Sec. III)
\[ \beta_i=\sqrt{I}\,\alpha_i+w,\qquad \alpha_0=\alpha,\ \alpha_1=-\alpha,\quad w\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2) \]
  • \(I\)relative irradiance, \(\mathbb E[I]=1\)
  • \(w\)additive Gaussian receiver noise
📖 Plain-language explanation for beginners
The two bits are two laser pulses of equal strength and opposite phase. The channel makes the pulse brighten or dim at random and adds noise.

The Gamma-Gamma channel and the Rytov variance

Rytov variance and Gamma-Gamma parameters
\[ \sigma_R^2=1.23\,C_n^2\,k^{7/6}L^{11/6},\quad k=\tfrac{2\pi}{\lambda},\qquad \alpha_{GG}=\Bigl[e^{\frac{0.49\sigma_R^2}{(1+1.11\sigma_R^{12/5})^{7/6}}}-1\Bigr]^{-1} \]
  • \(C_n^2\)atmospheric turbulence strength
  • \(\sigma_R^{12/5}\)written \((\sigma_R^2)^{6/5}\) in code: a power of \(\sigma_R^2\)
  • \(\beta_{GG}\)similar with 0.51, 0.69, 5/6
RememberThe scintillation index is \(\sigma_I^2=\tfrac1{\alpha_{GG}}+\tfrac1{\beta_{GG}}+\tfrac1{\alpha_{GG}\beta_{GG}}\). In very weak turbulence \(\alpha_{GG},\beta_{GG}\) reach the thousands and \(I\) is almost 1.

Example: \(\sigma_R^2=7\times10^{-5}\) gives \(\alpha_{GG}=2.915e+04\), \(\beta_{GG}=2.801e+04\) (recomputed, standard Andrews-Phillips form).

📖 Plain-language explanation for beginners
The Rytov variance is a number measuring how much light "shakes" after crossing turbulent air. The shape of the irradiance distribution follows from it.

The averaged density matrix and parity symmetry

The received state is a mixture
\[ \bar\rho_i=\mathbb E_{I,w}\bigl[\,|\beta_i\rangle\langle\beta_i|\,\bigr]=\iint f_I(I)\,\phi_\sigma(w)\,|\sqrt I\alpha_i+w\rangle\langle\sqrt I\alpha_i+w|\,dI\,dw \]
  • \(f_I\)Gamma-Gamma density
  • \(\phi_\sigma\)Gaussian noise density
BPSK parity symmetry
\[ \bar\rho_1=\Pi\,\bar\rho_0\,\Pi,\qquad \Pi=\mathrm{diag}\bigl((-1)^n\bigr),\quad [\bar\rho_1]_{mn}=(-1)^{m+n}[\bar\rho_0]_{mn} \]
  • \(\Pi\)photon-parity operator

Sampling both bits with the SAME \(I\) and \(w\) makes the symmetry exact up to rounding; independent sampling gives it only for very many samples.

📖 Plain-language explanation for beginners
Since the receiver does not know the irradiance and noise each time, it "sees" the average over every possibility. That average is a mixed state.

Cost table: SDP, oracle, closed form and surrogate

Cost of the ways to get \(P_e\) for two hypotheses
\[ \underbrace{O(M^2d^6)}_{\text{SDP, per iteration}}\quad\gg\quad \underbrace{O(Kd^2+d^3)}_{\text{quadrature + eig}}\quad\gg\quad \underbrace{O(fh+N_{\ell}h^2+hd^2+d^3)}_{\text{surrogate}} \]
  • \(M,d\)hypotheses, Fock dimension
  • \(K=n_In_w=1968\)quadrature nodes

📖 Plain-language explanation for beginners
Four ways to answer the same question, at very different speeds. The right column was measured on a Kaggle CPU machine.

The physics-constrained surrogate architecture

Two routes
\[ x\mapsto\hat P_e\quad(\text{direct}),\qquad x\mapsto(\hat\rho_0,\hat\rho_1)\mapsto\hat P_e\quad(\text{operator-level}) \]
  • \(x\)input feature vector
📖 Plain-language explanation for beginners
Instead of guessing the error probability directly, the main model predicts two density matrices and a fixed layer computes the error from them.

Physics features and their derivation chain

Physics feature vector
\[ x_{\rm phys}=(|\alpha|,\sigma,L,C_n^2,\lambda,\ k,\sigma_R^2,\alpha_{GG},\beta_{GG}),\qquad (C_n^2,\lambda,L)\mapsto(k,\sigma_R^2)\mapsto(\alpha_{GG},\beta_{GG}) \]
  • \(x_{\rm phys}\)9 features, 7 informative since \(\lambda,k\) are constant
  • \(\log\)\(C_n^2,\sigma_R^2,\alpha_{GG},\beta_{GG}\) are \(\log_{10}\)-scaled
z-score standardisation (training statistics only)
\[ \tilde x_j=\frac{x_j-\mu_j^{\rm train}}{s_j^{\rm train}} \]
Name in codeFeatures
BASEalpha_abs, sigma_noise, distance_L, Cn2, wavelength_lambda
TURBwave_number_k, rytov_variance, gamma_gamma_alpha, gamma_gamma_beta
slides9BASE + TURB
📖 Plain-language explanation for beginners
A small network struggles to learn the 7/6 and 11/6 powers, so the Rytov variance is computed beforehand and fed in as an input.

The compact (32, 64) SiLU backbone

MLP backbone
\[ z^{(\ell)}=\phi\!\bigl(W^{(\ell)}z^{(\ell-1)}+b^{(\ell)}\bigr),\quad z^{(0)}=\tilde x,\quad \phi=\mathrm{SiLU},\ (32,64) \]
  • \(\mathrm{SiLU}(u)\)\(u\,\sigma(u)\)
Parameter count (by formula)
\[ \underbrace{9\cdot32+32}_{320}+\underbrace{32\cdot64+64}_{2112}+2\underbrace{(64\cdot256+256)}_{16640}=35712 \]
  • \(2\times\)two heads \(\mathrm{Linear}(64\to d^2)\), \(d=16\)

The large backbone \((128,128,256,256)\) has 248192 parameters; the draft reports that it does not close the gap between feature sets.

📖 Plain-language explanation for beginners
The network has only about 36 thousand parameters. Most sit in the two output layers that produce the 16 by 16 matrices.

Cholesky density head: valid density matrices by construction

Cholesky density head
\[ \hat\rho_i=\frac{T_iT_i^{\top}}{\operatorname{Tr}(T_iT_i^{\top})},\qquad T_{jj}=\mathrm{softplus}(u_{jj})+10^{-5},\ T_{j\gt k}=u_{jk},\ T_{j\lt k}=0 \]
  • \(u\)raw output of the \(\mathrm{Linear}\) layer
  • \(\mathrm{softplus}\)\(\ln(1+e^{u})>0\)
Guaranteed by construction\(TT^{\top}\) is symmetric positive semidefinite and dividing by the trace gives \(\operatorname{Tr}\hat\rho=1\). No Hermiticity, trace or PSD penalty is needed.

Two separate heads for \(\rho_0,\rho_1\) do not impose \(\rho_1=\Pi\rho_0\Pi\); the \(\mathtt{ParityCholeskyHead}\) variant does and halves the parameters but is not the main model.

📖 Plain-language explanation for beginners
Any table of numbers multiplied by its transpose gives a non-negative matrix. Dividing by the diagonal sum makes the probabilities total 1.

The Helstrom layer: computed, not learned

Differentiable Helstrom layer
\[ \hat\Delta=\tfrac12(\hat\rho_1-\hat\rho_0),\ \ \tilde\Delta=\tfrac12(\hat\Delta+\hat\Delta^{\dagger}),\ \ \hat P_e=\operatorname{clip}_{[0,\frac12]}\Bigl[\tfrac12\Bigl(1-\sum_j|\lambda_j(\tilde\Delta)|\Bigr)\Bigr] \]
  • \(\lambda_j\)eigenvalues from \(\mathtt{eigvalsh}\)
  • \(\operatorname{clip}\)clamps to \([0,0.5]\) against finite-precision excursions
The gradient needs only eigenvalues
\[ \frac{\partial\lambda_i}{\partial\Delta_{jk}}=u_i[j]\,u_i[k],\qquad \partial|\lambda|=\operatorname{sign}(\lambda)\ \ (\partial|0|=0) \]

The layer has no trainable parameters but passes gradients back into both heads. The gradient has no \(1/(\lambda_i-\lambda_j)\) term since only eigenvalues are needed, so the repeated zero eigenvalues of a low-rank \(\hat\Delta\) cause no singularity.

📖 Plain-language explanation for beginners
The step "from two matrices to the error probability" has an exact formula, so it stays a fixed layer and only the unknown part is learned.

Loss: state Frobenius plus Helstrom error

Loss of the operator-level route
\[ \mathcal L_{\rm state}=\tfrac1N\sum_n\bigl[\|\hat\rho_0^{(n)}-\rho_{0,\rm ref}^{(n)}\|_F^2+\|\hat\rho_1^{(n)}-\rho_{1,\rm ref}^{(n)}\|_F^2\bigr],\quad \mathcal L_{\rm H}=\tfrac1N\sum_n\bigl(\hat P_e^{(n)}-P_{e,\rm ref}^{(n)}\bigr)^2 \]
  • \(\|\cdot\|_F\)Frobenius norm
  • \(P_{e,\rm ref}\)label from the quadrature oracle
Total loss with fixed weight
\[ \mathcal L_{\rm op}=\mathcal L_{\rm state}+\lambda\,\mathcal L_{\rm H},\qquad \lambda=1\ \ (\text{GradNorm off})\]
Default-constant trapThe function \(\mathtt{total\_loss}\) once defaulted to 0.1 but every call site passes \(\lambda\) explicitly from \(\mathtt{TrainConfig}\) (default 1.0). The 0.1 value never affected any checkpoint and was set to 1.0 (5 Oct 2026).
📖 Plain-language explanation for beginners
The network is penalised twice: if the two matrices are wrong and if the error probability derived from them is wrong.

The quadrature oracle: deterministic labels, no sampling

Quadrature oracle (Sec. V-A)
\[ \hat\rho_i=\sum_{k=1}^{n_I}\sum_{l=1}^{n_w}w_k^{(I)}w_l^{(w)}f(I_k)\phi(w_l)\,|\sqrt{I_k}\alpha_i+w_l\rangle\langle\sqrt{I_k}\alpha_i+w_l| \]
  • \(n_I=48\)Gauss-Legendre nodes in \(\log I\)
  • \(n_w=41\)Gauss-Hermite nodes over noise
Normalisation after Fock truncation
\[ \hat\rho_i\leftarrow\frac{\hat\rho_i}{\operatorname{Tr}\hat\rho_i},\qquad \langle n|\beta\rangle=e^{-|\beta|^2/2}\frac{\beta^n}{\sqrt{n!}},\ \ n\lt d=16 \]

The node count is \(K=48\times41=1968\); nodes are shared across the two bits so \(\rho_1=\Pi\rho_0\Pi\) holds to rounding. The \(\log I\) window shrinks with the width of the density (\(\pm10\) standard deviations) so nodes are not lost when \(\alpha_{GG},\beta_{GG}\) are large.

📖 Plain-language explanation for beginners
Instead of rolling random numbers hundreds of times and averaging, the average is taken on a fixed grid of points, accurate and repeatable.

The cell-stratified dataset, stratified_v3

Cells and sample counts
\[ 10\times11=110\ \text{\text{cells}}/\sigma,\quad 3\sigma\Rightarrow330\ \text{\text{cells}};\quad 303\times300=90.900\ \text{train},\ 152\times100=15.200\ \text{test} \]
  • \(\bar n\in[0.1;1.1]\)10 bins of width 0.1
  • \(\log_{10}\sigma_R^2\in[-5.7;0.9]\)11 bins of width 0.6
📖 Plain-language explanation for beginners
The parameter plane is cut into cells; every cell has training data, except a 3 by 3 block per noise level kept aside.

Worked numbers: from raw outputs to a density matrix

A \(2\times2\) Cholesky head step by step
\[ T=\begin{pmatrix}0.9741&0\\ 0.3&0.5981\end{pmatrix},\quad TT^{\top}=\begin{pmatrix}0.9488&0.2922\\ 0.2922&0.4478\end{pmatrix},\quad \operatorname{Tr}=1.3966 \]
  • \(u_{11},u_{22},u_{21}\)\(0.5,\,-0.2,\,0.3\)
  • \(T_{jj}\)\(\mathrm{softplus}(0.5)+10^{-5},\ \mathrm{softplus}(-0.2)+10^{-5}\)
Density matrix and eigenvalues
\[ \hat\rho=\begin{pmatrix}0.6794&0.2092\\ 0.2092&0.3206\end{pmatrix},\quad \operatorname{Tr}\hat\rho=1,\quad \lambda=\{0.2244,\,0.7756\}\ge0 \]

Both eigenvalues are positive even though the raw output \(u_{22}=-0.2\) is negative: the \(\mathrm{softplus}\) on the diagonal ensures it.

📖 Plain-language explanation for beginners
Three raw numbers are arranged in a lower-triangular matrix, multiplied by its transpose and divided by the trace. The result is always a valid state.

Worked numbers: the Helstrom layer on a 2 by 2 matrix

Helstrom layer for \(\rho_1=\Pi\rho_0\Pi\)
\[ \rho_0=\begin{pmatrix}0.7&0.2\\0.2&0.3\end{pmatrix},\ \rho_1=\begin{pmatrix}0.7&-0.2\\-0.2&0.3\end{pmatrix},\ \hat\Delta=\tfrac12(\rho_1-\rho_0)=\begin{pmatrix}0&-0.2\\-0.2&0\end{pmatrix} \]
  • \(\Pi\)\(\mathrm{diag}(1,-1)\)
Eigenvalues, trace norm and \(P_e\)
\[ \lambda=\{-0.2,\,0.2\},\quad \|\hat\Delta\|_1=0.4,\quad \hat P_e=\tfrac12(1-0.4)=0.30 \]
  • \(\sum|\lambda|\)sum of absolute eigenvalues

Check with \(\mathtt{numpy.linalg.eigvalsh}\): \(\lambda=[-0.2, 0.2]\), \(P_e=0.30\). (illustration, a self-chosen 2 by 2 matrix)

📖 Plain-language explanation for beginners
The two matrices differ only in the sign of the off-diagonal entries. Their difference has eigenvalues plus and minus 0.2, so the error probability is 0.3.

Training cost and the break-even point

Break-even of the training cost
\[ N^{*}=\frac{T_{\rm train}}{t_{\rm ref}-t_{\rm query}},\quad T_{\rm train}\approx1.1\times10^{5}\,\text{s}\Rightarrow N^{*}_{\rm SDP}\approx9.7\times10^{5},\ \ N^{*}_{\rm oracle}\approx8.2\times10^{6} \]
  • \(t_{\rm ref}\)114 ms (SDP) or 13.5 ms (oracle)
  • \(t_{\rm query}\)\(0.018\) ms
Sweeping one million design points
\[ 10^{6}\times114\,\text{ms}=31.7\,\text{h},\quad 10^{6}\times13.5\,\text{ms}=3.75\,\text{h},\quad 10^{6}\times0.018\,\text{ms}=18\,\text{s} \]
Batch versus per-call trapThe 0.018 ms advantage holds only when batching 256. One call at a time costs 0.79 ms due to fixed call overhead, so against the oracle the gain is only about 17 times.
📖 Plain-language explanation for beginners
Training costs once, about 30 CPU hours. After about a million queries the surrogate is cheaper than calling the SDP.

Two validity indicators

The unconverged-label trap at large noise

Relative label error
\[ \Bigl|\frac{P_e^{\rm label}-P_e^{\rm conv}}{P_e^{\rm conv}}\Bigr|=\frac{|0.3608-0.3848|}{0.3848}\approx6.24\% \]
  • \(P_e^{\rm label}\)\(n_w=7\)
  • \(P_e^{\rm conv}\)\(n_w=61\)
Trap: small MSE against wrong labelsA surrogate trained on \(n_w=7\) labels reached an MSE of about \(10^{-8}\) against the old labels but about \(2.7\times10^{-5}\) against converged labels (stratified_v3). Every v2 number is now only a reference.
Case thật · Deep audit of stratified_v2, 7 Oct 2026

Stored labels match the eigenvalues of the float32 \(\rho\) (at most \(2\times10^{-8}\) off) and no sample is shared across splits, but the \(n_w=7\) noise quadrature is not converged at \(\sigma=1\). Relabelling all 127,760 samples took 82 s on a laptop; \(n_I=48\), \(n_w=41\), \(d=16\) stay.

Source: ttn-12-08/EXPERIMENTS.md, section "Deep audit of stratified_v2"
📖 Plain-language explanation for beginners
A model can fit a wrong answer perfectly. So the answer key itself must be checked before trusting the score.

The closed form Eq. (39): coverage and accuracy

Closed-form coverage
\[ \frac{1765}{15200}=11.6\%,\qquad \text{weak: }\frac{87}{12498}=0.7\%,\ \text{medium: }\frac{657}{1303}=50.4\%,\ \text{strong: }\frac{1021}{1399}=73.0\% \]
  • \(\text{FP64}\)direct double-precision evaluation

Error by turbulence regime

Partition by Rytov variance
\[ \text{weak: }\sigma_R^2\lt0.5,\quad\text{medium: }0.5\le\sigma_R^2\lt2.0,\quad\text{strong: }\sigma_R^2\ge2.0 \]
📖 Plain-language explanation for beginners
The stronger the turbulence, the harder the two bits are to separate, so the model error also grows steadily, though within one order of magnitude.

Held-out cells and held-out noise levels

The weak point: interpolation in \(\sigma\)
\[ \frac{7.7\times10^{-6}}{2.0\times10^{-9}}\approx3.8e+03\ \ (\sigma\in\{0.2,0.3,0.75\}\ \text{so với test}) \]
  • \(\sigma\)trained only at 0.1, 0.5, 1.0
Noise extrapolation trapWith three trained noise levels the network has no basis for interpolating in \(\sigma\); treat a continuous noise level as outside the validity range. More training did not help (about \(7\times10^{-6}\) through two resume rounds).

Probes beyond the trained range: the surrogate loses for \(\bar n\ge3\)

Who wins by \(\bar n\)
\[ \text{ratio}=\frac{\rm MSE_{CF}}{\rm MSE_{sur}}:\quad 4.1\times10^{6}\ (\text{in range}),\ 11\ (\bar n\in[2,3]),\ 0.35\ (\bar n\in[3,4]) \]
📖 Plain-language explanation for beginners
The surrogate beats the closed form for weak signals, but at strong signals (tiny error probability) the closed form wins.

The lookup-table baseline: the strongest rival

Lookup table versus surrogate
\[ \frac{\mathrm{MSE}_{\rm sur}}{\mathrm{MSE}_{\rm A}}=\frac{2.0\times10^{-9}}{4.9\times10^{-10}}\approx4.1,\qquad G=g^{k},\ \ 3900\ \text{\text{oracle calls}}\approx20\,\text{s} \]
  • \(G\)grid points, growing as a power of the inputs
Fair-comparison trapFor a problem with 3 effective inputs (\(\bar n,\sigma,\sigma_R^2\)) a table built in 20 s already matches or beats the surrogate in error and speed (about 0.15 \(\mu\)s). The surrogate advantage appears only with more inputs.

📖 Plain-language explanation for beginners
With only three variables an interpolated table is enough and fast. A neural network is worthwhile only when the number of variables makes a table infeasible.

The parity residual and the error structure

Parity residual

Detailed notes

Theory and historical background

Helstrom (1969, 1976) gave the minimum error of two-state discrimination; Holevo (1973) and Eldar, Megretski and Verghese (2003) cast it as POVM optimisation and SDP. The turbulent channel uses the Gamma-Gamma model (Al-Habash 2001, Andrews and Phillips 2005). Bhatnagar, Arti and Bhatnagar (2025) give the closed form Eq. (39). The current paper replaces the costly label chain by a surrogate: channel parameters in, a valid density matrix out, and a Helstrom layer computing \(P_e\).

Plain words, technique, table, calculation

Plain words: ask how often a laser link through rough air errs; solving exactly is slow, so a network is taught to guess, but forced to guess through valid matrices. Technique: \(x\mapsto\hat\rho_{0,1}\mapsto\hat P_e\) with a Cholesky head and a Helstrom layer. Table: the "Comparison table" slide. Calculation: the examples below.

Example · Pure BPSK (quantum optics)

\(\bar n=0.5\): \(e^{-4\bar n}=e^{-2}=0.1353\), \(P_e=\tfrac12(1-\sqrt{1-0.1353})=0.03506\). This is the floor of any receiver without turbulence.

Example · Rytov variance (meteorology, illustration)

\(C_n^2=5\cdot10^{-14}\), \(\lambda=1.55\,\mu\)m, \(L=1200\) m: \(k=4.054×106\) m\(^{-1}\), \(\sigma_R^2=1.23\,C_n^2k^{7/6}L^{11/6}=1.391\): moderate turbulence; on a clear night \(C_n^2\) is tens of times smaller.

Example · Cholesky head (machine learning)

Raw outputs \((0.5,-0.2,0.3)\) give \(\operatorname{Tr}(TT^\top)=1.3966\), \(\hat\rho=\begin{pmatrix}0.6794&0.2092\\0.2092&0.3206\end{pmatrix}\), eigenvalues \(0.2244\) and \(0.7756\): valid although \(u_{22}\lt0\).

Example · Helstrom layer (telecommunications)

With \(\rho_0,\rho_1\) of the example slide, \(\hat\Delta\) has eigenvalues \(\pm0.2\), \(\|\hat\Delta\|_1=0.4\), \(P_e=0.30\): the optimal receiver still errs on 3 of 10 bits. (illustration)

Example · Design sweep (systems engineering)

A grid of \(40\cdot3\cdot30\cdot30=108000\) points: SDP \(3.4\) h, oracle \(24\) min, batched surrogate \(1.9\) s. (assumption: self-chosen grid)

Example · Training break-even (computing economics)

\(T=1.1\times10^5\) s, \(t_{\rm SDP}-t_{\rm query}=0.114\) s: \(N^*=9.65\times10^{5}\approx10^6\) queries. Below this, calling the oracle directly is cheaper.

Case thật · How the draft marks provisional numbers

Section V-C states that four resume rounds (about 10,000 epochs) of Phys-log have not brought any seed to the patience rule, test MSE lies in \(0.8\) to \(1.3\times10^{-9}\) and still falls slowly; the main tables are therefore not converged values.

Pitfalls

  • Confusing \(|\alpha|\) and \(|\alpha|^2=\bar n\) in \(e^{-4|\alpha|^2}\).
  • Forgetting the \(\tfrac12\) in \(\hat\Delta=\tfrac12(\hat\rho_1-\hat\rho_0)\) makes \(P_e\) negative or wrong.
  • Reporting a small MSE against unconverged labels.
  • Reporting MSE only on the samples where the closed form is finite.
  • Using the surrogate outside \(\bar n\le1.1\) or the three noise levels.
