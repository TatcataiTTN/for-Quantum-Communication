The four-state coherent constellation

QPSK constellation
\[ \alpha_k=\sqrt{\bar n}\,e^{i\pi k/2},\qquad |\alpha_k|^2=\bar n,\qquad k=0,1,2,3 \]
  • \(\bar n\)mean photons per symbol
  • \(k\)symbol index, each step a 90° turn
📖 Plain-language explanation for beginners
Four laser pulses of equal strength but phases 0°, 90°, 180°, 270°. Each symbol carries 2 bits.

Coherent states and photon statistics

Fock expansion and Poisson statistics
\[ |\alpha\rangle=e^{-|\alpha|^2/2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^n}{\sqrt{n!}}|n\rangle,\qquad P(n)=e^{-\bar n}\frac{\bar n^{\,n}}{n!} \]
  • \(|n\rangle\)n-photon state
  • \(P(n)\)probability of counting n photons

From \(\hat a|\alpha\rangle=\alpha|\alpha\rangle\) we get \(\bar n=\langle\alpha|\hat a^\dagger\hat a|\alpha\rangle=|\alpha|^2\) and \(\mathrm{Var}(n)=\bar n\). For QPSK, \(\alpha_k=\sqrt{\bar n}\,i^k\), so all four symbols share one Poisson law and differ only in the phase of each Fock amplitude.

The counted photons fluctuate around n̄ like raindrops: mean n̄, spread √n̄.

Overlaps between states

The general rule \(|\langle\alpha_j|\alpha_k\rangle|^2=e^{-|\alpha_j-\alpha_k|^2}\) gives \(|\alpha_0-\alpha_1|^2=2\bar n\) and \(|\alpha_0-\alpha_2|^2=4\bar n\).

📖 Plain-language explanation for beginners
Two states that sit close on the circle look alike and are easily confused.

The Gram matrix of the four states

Circulant Gram matrix
\[ G_{jk}=\langle\alpha_j|\alpha_k\rangle=\exp\!\bigl[\bar n\,(i^{\,k-j}-1)\bigr]=g_{(k-j)\bmod 4} \]
  • \(g_m\)first row of G; later rows are cyclic shifts
  • \(g_0=1\)normalisation

Since \(G_{jk}\) depends only on \((k-j)\bmod 4\), \(G\) is circulant: \(g_1=e^{\bar n(i-1)}\), \(g_2=e^{-2\bar n}\), \(g_3=\overline{g_1}\). Moduli: \(|g_1|=e^{-\bar n}\), \(|g_2|=e^{-2\bar n}\).

Density matrices in the Fock basis

Z4 symmetry of density matrices
\[ \rho_k=U^{k}\rho_0U^{-k},\quad U=e^{i\pi\hat n/2},\quad \rho_k(m,n)=e^{-\bar n}\frac{\bar n^{(m+n)/2}}{\sqrt{m!\,n!}}\,e^{i\pi k(m-n)/2} \]
  • \(U\)90° phase rotation, diagonal in the Fock basis
  • \(\rho_k(m,n)\)Fock-basis density-matrix element

The diagonal \(\rho_k(n,n)=e^{-\bar n}\bar n^n/n!\) is the same for all \(k\); only the phase \(e^{i\pi k(m-n)/2}\) tells symbols apart. \(\mathrm{Tr}\,\rho_k=1\), \(\mathrm{Tr}\,\rho_k^2=1\).

Mapping bits to symbols

Spectral efficiency is \(\eta=\log_2 4=2\) bit/symbol, twice BPSK. With Gray labels, neighbours differ in one bit so \(\mathrm{BER}\approx\mathrm{SER}/2\).

📖 Plain-language explanation for beginners
Gray means neighbouring labels differ in one bit, so confusing a neighbour corrupts only one bit.

The Helstrom problem for four states

Minimum-error discrimination
\[ P_e^{\min}=\min_{\{\Pi_k\}}\Bigl\{1-\frac14\sum_{k=0}^{3}\mathrm{Tr}(\Pi_k\rho_k)\Bigr\},\quad \Pi_k\succeq0,\ \sum_k\Pi_k=I \]
  • \(\Pi_k\)POVM element: the verdict "symbol k was sent"
  • \(1/4\)equal priors
📖 Plain-language explanation for beginners
We pick the measurement that guesses the symbol correctly most often; it is a convex optimisation.

SDP duality and optimality conditions

Duality and the Holevo condition
\[ \max\ \mathrm{Tr}\,Y\ \ \text{s.t.}\ Y\preceq\tfrac14\rho_k\ \forall k,\qquad P_e^{\min}=1-\mathrm{Tr}\,Y^\star \]
  • \(Y\)Hermitian dual variable
  • \(Y^\star\)optimiser
Optimality conditions (KKT)
\[ Y=\tfrac14\sum_k\rho_k\Pi_k=Y^\dagger,\qquad Y-\tfrac14\rho_k\succeq0,\qquad \Pi_k\bigl(\tfrac14\rho_k-Y\bigr)=0 \]
  • \(\Pi_k(\tfrac14\rho_k-Y)=0\)complementary slackness: Π_k lives only where ρ_k is strong enough

The document writes \(P_e^{\min}=1-4\,\mathrm{Tr}\,Y^\star\) (Eq. 61); with the constraint \(Y\preceq\tfrac14\rho_k\) the correct form is \(1-\mathrm{Tr}\,Y^\star\).

📖 Plain-language explanation for beginners
If a measurement satisfies the Holevo condition, no other measurement can do better.

Square-root measurement for pure states

Square-root (pretty good) measurement
\[ S=\sum_{k}\rho_k,\qquad \Pi_k=S^{-1/2}\rho_kS^{-1/2},\qquad P_c=\frac14\sum_k\mathrm{Tr}(\Pi_k\rho_k) \]
  • \(S\)sum of the density matrices
  • \(S^{-1/2}\)inverse square root on the support of S

For pure states \(\rho_k=|\psi_k\rangle\langle\psi_k|\) with Gram matrix \(G\), what matters is \(\sqrt G\): \(P_c=\frac14\sum_k\bigl[(\sqrt G)_{kk}\bigr]^2\). Only a \(4\times4\) matrix is needed, no Fock basis.

When it appliesThe \(\sqrt G\) formula holds only for pure states. With noise, \(\tilde\rho_k\) are mixed.
📖 Plain-language explanation for beginners
The PGM is the easy-to-compute measurement: sum the states, then normalise with an inverse square root.

The PGM is optimal thanks to Z4 symmetry

PGM under Z4 symmetry
\[ G=\sum_{l=0}^{3}\lambda_l\,|f_l\rangle\langle f_l|,\quad \lambda_l=4p_l,\quad P_e=1-\frac14\Bigl(\sum_{l=0}^{3}\sqrt{p_l}\Bigr)^{2} \]
  • \(|f_l\rangle\)fixed Fourier vectors, independent of n̄
  • \(p_l\)probability that n lies in class n mod 4 = l

Because \(G\) is circulant, its eigenvalues are the Fourier transform of the first row, and the PGM reaches the Helstrom bound (Eldar and Forney, 2001: geometrically uniform states). Numerical check: \(\lambda_{\min}(Y-\tfrac14\rho_k)\approx -10^{-17}\) for \(\bar n\in\{0.5,1,2\}\), so the Holevo condition holds.

📖 Plain-language explanation for beginners
The four states are rotated copies of each other, so the natural symmetric measurement is also the best one.

Closed form of P_e via four class probabilities

The four photon classes
\[ p_l=\sum_{n\equiv l\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 4)}e^{-\bar n}\frac{\bar n^{\,n}}{n!}:\quad p_{0,2}=\tfrac12e^{-\bar n}(\cosh\bar n\pm\cos\bar n),\ \ p_{1,3}=\tfrac12e^{-\bar n}(\sinh\bar n\pm\sin\bar n) \]
  • \(p_0+p_1+p_2+p_3=1\)normalisation
  • \(\cosh,\sinh,\cos,\sin\)Taylor sums over the fourth roots of unity

Tail approximation of the error probability

Pairwise union estimate
\[ P_e\approx\underbrace{4}_{\text{adjacent pairs}}\cdot\frac12\cdot\frac14\,|g_1|^2=\frac12\,e^{-2\bar n},\qquad P_e^{\mathrm{BPSK}}\approx\frac14\,e^{-4\bar n} \]
  • \(\tfrac14|g_1|^2\)Helstrom error of one pair at priors 1/2 each
  • \(\tfrac12\)weight of the two states in the pair (1/4+1/4)

The ratio \(P_e^{\mathrm{PGM}}/(\tfrac12e^{-2\bar n})\) is 1.043 at \(\bar n=2\), 1.00034 at \(\bar n=3\) and 1.0000011 at \(\bar n=6\).

📖 Plain-language explanation for beginners
For large n̄ almost every error is a mix-up with an immediate neighbour; each adjacent pair adds a fixed small amount.

Why the exponent differs
\[ |\langle\alpha_0|\alpha_1\rangle|^2=e^{-2\bar n}\ \Rightarrow\ P_e\sim e^{-2\bar n},\ \text{not}\ e^{-\bar n}=|\langle\alpha_0|\alpha_1\rangle| \]

Additive noise makes the states mixed

Additive noise gives a displaced thermal state
\[ \bar\rho_k=\mathbb E_\epsilon|\beta+\epsilon\rangle\langle\beta+\epsilon|=D(\beta)\,\rho_{\mathrm{th}}(\sigma^2)\,D(\beta)^\dagger,\quad \mathrm{Tr}\,\bar\rho_k^2=\frac1{1+2\sigma^2} \]
  • \(\rho_{\mathrm{th}}(\sigma^2)\)thermal state with n̄_th = σ²
  • \(\epsilon\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,\sigma^2)\)complex noise

Check: purity 0.83333 at \(\sigma^2=0.1\) and 0.625 at \(\sigma^2=0.3\), matching \(1/(1+2\sigma^2)\). The "pure" approximation of Eq. (41) therefore holds only for \(\sigma^2\ll1\).

📖 Plain-language explanation for beginners
Noise does not just shift the state, it blurs it, so it is no longer pure.

SDP cost and the benefit of symmetry

Computational cost
\[ T_{\mathrm{SDP}}=\mathcal O(N^3M)\ \text{per sample},\qquad T_{\mathrm{Z4}}=\mathcal O(1)\ \text{(a }4\times4\text{ Gram matrix)} \]

The relay shortens the link

Rytov ratio of direct link and one hop
\[ \frac{\sigma^2_{R,\mathrm{direct}}}{\sigma^2_{R,\mathrm{hop}}}=\frac{(2L)^{11/6}}{L^{11/6}}=2^{11/6}\approx 3.564 \]

Detailed notes

Theory and background

Minimum-error discrimination among several quantum states rests on Helstrom (1976), Holevo (1973) and Yuen–Lax (1973). Two states have a closed form; four states in general need an SDP. What is special about coherent QPSK is that the states \(\{|\alpha i^k\rangle\}\) are rotated copies of each other, a geometrically uniform set: Eldar and Forney (2001) showed the square-root measurement is then optimal. An \(N^2\)-variable SDP therefore collapses to four class probabilities \(p_l\). The lab’s documents (January 2026) extend the 82-step frame of the BPSK paper (Bhatnagar et al., 2025) to QPSK but still treat QPSK as "without closed form"; the checks here show a closed form exists for pure states and serves as the reference for any approximation.

Plain words first, then the technique

Plain words: the four symbols are the four compass points (N, E, S, W) with arrows of equal length. The receiver must guess which way the arrow points through a measurement blurred by quantum mechanics. Since the four directions are symmetric, the fairest guess is also the best. Technique: a circulant Gram matrix, diagonalised by Fourier vectors, with four eigenvalues \(4p_l\).

Worked examples, step by step

Ví dụ · 1. Overlaps at n̄ = 0.5 (quantum FSO link)
  1. \(|\alpha_0-\alpha_1|^2=2\bar n=1\), \(|\alpha_0-\alpha_2|^2=4\bar n=2\).
  2. Squared overlaps: adjacent \(e^{-1}=\)0.3679, opposite \(e^{-2}=\)0.1353.
  3. Neighbouring states already overlap strongly: BPSK at the same n̄ has only 0.135 for its pair.
Ví dụ · 2. From four class probabilities to P_e (n̄ = 1)
  1. Compute the four probabilities: 0.3832, 0.3709, 0.1845, 0.06139.
  2. Roots: 0.619, 0.6091, 0.4295, 0.2478.
  3. Sum 1.9053, squared 3.6303.
  4. P_e = 1 − squared/4 = 0.092421.
Ví dụ · 3. Power budget for a satellite-to-ground link (illustration)
  1. Assume λ = 1.55 µm: photon energy hν = 1.282×10⁻¹⁹ J.
  2. Assume 1 Gbaud. The target P_e = 10⁻³ needs n̄ = 3.107 for QPSK.
  3. Mean received power: 0.3982 nW (n̄ times hν times symbol rate), for 2 Gbit/s.
  4. BPSK at 2 Gbaud needs n̄ = 1.381 and 0.3539 nW for the same 2 Gbit/s: less power than QPSK but twice the bandwidth.
Ví dụ · 4. Laser phase tolerance (coherent optical link, illustration)
  1. Assume the link needs P_e ≤ 10⁻⁴ at n̄ = 6 (no mismatch gives 3.07×10-6).
  2. At 1° mismatch: P_e = 0.00183, already 18 times the target.
  3. At 5°: 0.0446. Conclusion: a phase-locked loop comes before adding photons.
Ví dụ · 5. Decode-and-forward relay at n̄ = 2
  1. Single-hop P_e = 0.009553.
  2. P₁+P₂−P₁P₂ = 0.019015; sum 2P_e = 0.019106.
  3. Exact via the confusion matrix: 0.018971, below both because a +1 then −1 error cancels.
Ví dụ · 6. Choosing the Fock dimension for a simulation (state recognition, illustration)
  1. The rule N = ⌈3n̄+10⌉ gives N = 40 at n̄ = 10.
  2. Dropped tail P(n≥N) = 7.34×10-13; the n̄+10√n̄ rule gives N = 42 and tail 4.20×10-14.
  3. Real variables of the N=25 SDP: 4N² = 2500 (as Step 36 lists).

Pitfalls when solving problems

Five frequent traps
  • Forgetting to square the overlap: \(|\langle\alpha_0|\alpha_1\rangle|=e^{-\bar n}\) yet the error follows \(e^{-2\bar n}\).
  • Mixing up \(|\alpha|\) and \(|\alpha|^2=\bar n\) in \(|\alpha_j-\alpha_k|^2\).
  • Using labels 00, 01, 10, 11 and claiming BER = SER/2; that needs Gray labels.
  • Applying the \(\sqrt G\) formula for \(P_c\) to noisy mixed states.
  • Evaluating \(2^{11/6}\) as 4.3 instead of 3.564.
