Telling two hypotheses apart with a single measurement

Total error probability
\[ P_e=\pi_0\,\mathrm{Tr}[E_1\rho_0]+\pi_1\,\mathrm{Tr}[E_0\rho_1] \]
  • \(\pi_0,\pi_1\)prior probabilities, summing to 1
  • \(E_0,E_1\)two POVM elements, \(E_0+E_1=\mathbb 1\)
Helstrom result (one copy)
\[ P_{e,\min}=\tfrac12\bigl(1-\|\pi_1\rho_1-\pi_0\rho_0\|_1\bigr) \]

Source: Audenaert et al., arXiv:quant-ph/0610027 (2007), "Problem formulation", p. 2. The optimal measurement projects onto the positive part of \(\pi_1\rho_1-\pi_0\rho_0\).

📖 Plain-language explanation for beginners
Two sources emit different states. We measure and then guess which one it was. The formula gives the smallest error of any measurement.

The classical Chernoff bound and the exponential error rate

Classical exponent
\[ P_e\sim e^{-N\,C(p_0,p_1)},\quad C=-\min_{s\in[0,1]}\ln\sum_b p_0^{s}(b)\,p_1^{1-s}(b) \]
  • \(N\)number of independent observations
  • \(C\)Chernoff information
📖 Plain-language explanation for beginners
The more observations, the faster the error falls exponentially. The number C says how fast.

The inequality min{p,q} ≤ p^s q^(1−s)

Key step
\[ \min\{p,q\}\le p^{s}q^{1-s},\quad 0\le s\le 1 \]
Consequence for N observations
\[ P_e\le\tfrac12\min_{s}\Bigl(\sum_b p_0^{s}(b)\,p_1^{1-s}(b)\Bigr)^{N} \]

If \(p\le q\) then \(q^{1-s}p^{s}\ge p^{s}p^{1-s}=p\). Sum over \(b\) and raise to the power \(N\) because the observations are independent (Calsamiglia et al., Eqs. (1)-(2), pp. 2 and 3).

📖 Plain-language explanation for beginners
The smaller of two numbers never exceeds a "blend" of them. We choose the best blend s.

A biased-coin example: the Chernoff information in numbers

A type-0 coin lands heads with probability \(p=0.3\), a type-1 coin with \(q=0.6\). Decision threshold and Chernoff information:

Two-outcome formula
\[ \xi=\frac{\ln(\bar q/\bar p)}{\ln(p/\bar p)+\ln(\bar q/q)}=0.4467,\qquad C=\xi\ln\frac{\xi}{p}+\bar\xi\ln\frac{\bar\xi}{\bar p}=0.04771 \]
  • \(\bar p,\bar q\)1−p, 1−q
  • \(s^*\)minimiser, equal to 0.494

The Hellinger arc and relative entropy

Hellinger arc
\[ p_s(b)=\frac{p_0^{s}(b)\,p_1^{1-s}(b)}{\sum_b p_0^{s}(b)\,p_1^{1-s}(b)} \]
Chernoff information via Kullback–Leibler
\[ C(p_0,p_1)=K(p_{s^*}\,\|\,p_0)=K(p_{s^*}\,\|\,p_1) \]
  • \(K(p\|q)\)\(\sum p\ln(p/q)\)
  • \(s^*\)the point equidistant from \(p_0\) and \(p_1\)

Source: Calsamiglia et al., arXiv:0708.2343 (2008), Eqs. (4)-(5), p. 3; Nussbaum, Szkoła, Ann. Statist. 37(2), 2009, Eqs. (4)-(5), p. 6.

📖 Plain-language explanation for beginners
The Hellinger arc is an "interpolation" path between two distributions. The optimum sits equally far from both ends.

Matrix powers of positive operators and the sum form of Q_s

Power through the spectrum
\[ \rho^{s}=\sum_i\lambda_i^{s}\,|i\rangle\langle i|,\qquad \lambda_i\gt0 \]
Sum form of \(\mathrm{Tr}\,\rho^s\sigma^{1-s}\)
\[ \mathrm{Tr}[\rho^{s}\sigma^{1-s}]=\sum_{i,j}\lambda_i^{s}\mu_j^{1-s}\,|U_{ij}|^{2},\quad \sigma=U\,\mathrm{diag}(\mu)\,U^{*} \]
  • \(\lambda_i\)eigenvalues of \(\rho\)
  • \(\mu_j\)eigenvalues of \(\sigma\)
  • \(|U_{ij}|^2\)doubly stochastic overlap of the two eigenbases

This is exactly how the widget at the end computes \(Q_s\): one Jacobi decomposition per matrix, then a sum. Source: Audenaert et al., arXiv:quant-ph/0610027 (2007), "Properties", p. 3.

📖 Plain-language explanation for beginners
To raise a matrix to the power s, diagonalise it and raise each eigenvalue to the power s.

Many copies: tensor products and N-copy Helstrom

Helstrom for \(N\) copies
\[ P_{e,\min,N}=\tfrac12\Bigl(1-\bigl\|\pi_1\rho_1^{\otimes N}-\pi_0\rho_0^{\otimes N}\bigr\|_1\Bigr) \]
Multiplicativity of the trace
\[ \mathrm{Tr}\bigl[(\rho^{\otimes N})^{s}(\sigma^{\otimes N})^{1-s}\bigr]=\bigl(\mathrm{Tr}[\rho^{s}\sigma^{1-s}]\bigr)^{N} \]

The \(d^N\times d^N\) matrix grows very fast, so the exact formula serves only small \(N\); the right side of the identity below needs only a \(d\times d\) matrix (Audenaert et al., p. 2; Calsamiglia et al., Eqs. (13) and (20)).

📖 Plain-language explanation for beginners
With N copies the matrix to handle blows up exponentially. But Q needs only one single-copy computation, then the N-th power.

Method

  • Definition of Q and ξ

Definition of the quantum Chernoff bound

Quantum Chernoff bound
\[ Q=\min_{0\le s\le1}\mathrm{Tr}[\rho^{s}\sigma^{1-s}],\quad \xi_{QCB}=-\ln Q \]
  • \(Q\)non-logarithmic form, \(0\le Q\le1\)
  • \(\xi_{QCB}\)optimal error exponent
📖 Plain-language explanation for beginners
Replace probabilities by density matrices and the quantum Chernoff bound appears. The result is surprisingly simple.

The Audenaert inequality: the central theorem

Consequence: an upper bound on the error
\[ A=\pi_1\rho_1^{\otimes n},\ B=\pi_0\rho_0^{\otimes n}\ \Rightarrow\ P_{e,\min,n}\le \pi_0^{s}\pi_1^{1-s}\bigl(\mathrm{Tr}\,\rho_0^{s}\rho_1^{1-s}\bigr)^{n} \]
📖 Plain-language explanation for beginners
The right side of the theorem is the optimal error (via the trace norm). The left side is an easy product. So the easy product bounds the error.

The upper bound ½Q^N for every pair of states

Chernoff bound for \(N\) copies
\[ P_e\le\tfrac12\,Q^{N}=\tfrac12\,e^{-N\xi_{QCB}} \]

For \(s=\tfrac12\) there is a short proof: pick \(\tilde E_1=\{\rho_0^{1/2}-\rho_1^{1/2}\lt0\}\) and obtain \(\mathrm{Tr}[\rho_0^{1/2}\rho_1^{1/2}]\) (Eq. (16)).

📖 Plain-language explanation for beginners
This is the "safe" statement: the actual error never exceeds half of Q to the power N.

The Nussbaum–Szkoła lower bound

The two bounds meet
\[ \lim_{n\to\infty}-\tfrac1n\ln P_{e,\min,n}=-\ln\min_{s}\mathrm{Tr}[\rho_0^{s}\rho_1^{1-s}] \]
📖 Plain-language explanation for beginners
One paper proves the error cannot fall faster than this; the other proves this rate is reached. Together they give the exact answer.

Proving the lower bound: reduction to a classical problem

Artificial classical laws
\[ p_{ij}=\lambda_i|\langle x_i|y_j\rangle|^{2},\quad q_{ij}=\gamma_j|\langle x_i|y_j\rangle|^{2} \]
Closing identity
\[ \sum_{i,j}p_{ij}^{1-s}q_{ij}^{s}=\mathrm{Tr}[\rho_0^{1-s}\rho_1^{s}] \]
📖 Plain-language explanation for beginners
Each pair of eigenvectors gives a "classical outcome". No quantum measurement beats the matching classical problem.

Convexity in s: a single minimum

Second derivative of the basic term
\[ \frac{d^{2}}{ds^{2}}\,x^{s}y^{1-s}=x^{s}y^{1-s}(\ln x-\ln y)^{2}\ge0 \]
The function to minimise is a convex combination
\[ Q_s=\sum_{i,j}\lambda_i^{s}\mu_j^{1-s}|U_{ij}|^{2},\quad |U_{ij}|^2\ge0 \]

Because \(Q_s\) is convex on \([0,1]\), a local minimum is the global one; golden-section or ternary search works (Audenaert et al., "Convexity in s", p. 3).

📖 Plain-language explanation for beginners
The graph of Q versus s is bowl-shaped: one bottom only. Find it and you are done, no false bottoms.

Sandwiching the trace distance with Q

Trace distance
\[ T(\rho,\sigma)=\tfrac12\|\rho-\sigma\|_1,\qquad 1-Q\le T\le\sqrt{1-Q^{2}} \]
  • \(T\)\(P_e^{\min}=\tfrac12(1-T)\) for equal priors

One-copy consequence: \(\tfrac12\bigl(1-\sqrt{1-Q^2}\bigr)\le P_e\le\tfrac12 Q\).

📖 Plain-language explanation for beginners
Knowing Q lets you pin the Helstrom error between two values without any trace norm.

Unequal priors

Weighted bound
\[ P_e\le\min_{s}\ \pi_0^{s}\pi_1^{1-s}\,\mathrm{Tr}[\rho_0^{s}\rho_1^{1-s}] \]
The exponent does not depend on \(\pi\)
\[ \lim_{n\to\infty}-\tfrac1n\ln P_{e,\min,n}=\xi_{QCB}\quad(\pi_0,\pi_1\gt0) \]

For pure BPSK, \(n=1\), \(\pi_0=0.8\): exact \(P_e=0.02214\), Chernoff bound \(=0.02707\), while equal priors give \(0.03506\). Source: Calsamiglia et al., Eq. (25), p. 5; Audenaert et al., arXiv:quant-ph/0610027 (2007), p. 2.

📖 Plain-language explanation for beginners
Whichever source we believe is more common, with many copies the rate of error decay is the same.

Fading averaging raises Q

Chain of inequalities
\[ Q(\bar\rho_0,\bar\rho_1)\ \ge\ \mathbb E_I\bigl[e^{-4\bar nI}\bigr]\ \ge\ e^{-4\bar n} \]
  • \(\mathbb E_I[\cdot]\)expectation over the Gamma-Gamma law, \(\mathbb E[I]=1\)
quantity (\(\sigma_R^2=1\), \(\bar n=0.5\))Q
\(e^{-4\bar n}\) (no fading)0.1353
\(\mathbb E_I[e^{-4\bar nI}]\)0.2717
\(Q(\bar\rho_0,\bar\rho_1)\) (with noise)0.3764
TrapReplacing \(I\) by its mean 1 gives \(Q=e^{-2}=0.135\), far below the real value. The error looks smaller only because fading was ignored.
📖 Plain-language explanation for beginners
When the intensity swings, the weak moments push the error up more than the strong moments pull it down.

Two fidelity conventions: the factor-2 trap

Two definitions
\[ F_{\mathrm{Aud}}=\mathrm{Tr}\,|\sqrt\rho\sqrt\sigma|,\qquad F_{\mathrm{Cal}}=F_{\mathrm{Aud}}^{\,2} \]
The same chain, two writings
\[ -\ln F_{\mathrm{Aud}}\le\xi_{QCB}\le-2\ln F_{\mathrm{Aud}}\iff-\tfrac12\ln F_{\mathrm{Cal}}\le\xi_{QCB}\le-\ln F_{\mathrm{Cal}} \]
conventionvalue of \(F\)lower endupper end

Some call fidelity the root overlap, others its square. Always check which one the paper uses.

Timeline from Chernoff to Calsamiglia

Milestones come from the reference lists of Audenaert et al., arXiv:quant-ph/0610027 (2007) and Calsamiglia et al., arXiv:0708.2343 (2008). Dashed: idea influence; solid: direct dependency. Formula: \(P_e\sim e^{-N\xi_{QCB}}\), \(\xi_{QCB}=-\ln\min_s\mathrm{Tr}\rho_0^s\rho_1^{1-s}\).

A recipe for Q on an arbitrary channel

Four steps
  1. Build \(\bar\rho_0,\bar\rho_1\) (quadrature) and normalise \(\mathrm{Tr}=1\).
  2. Diagonalise: \(\bar\rho_0=V\Lambda V^{\dagger}\), \(\bar\rho_1=W M W^{\dagger}\), form \(D_{ij}=|\langle v_i|w_j\rangle|^2\).
  3. Minimise over \(s\in(0,1)\) (convex function, ternary search).
  4. Read off \(\xi=-\ln Q\) and the two bounds \(\tfrac12(1-\sqrt{1-Q^2})\), \(\tfrac12Q\).
Function to minimise
\[ Q_s=\sum_{i,j}\lambda_i^{s}\mu_j^{1-s}D_{ij}\ \xrightarrow{\ \min_s\ }\ Q \]
Cost
\[ O(d^{3})\ +\ O(d^{2}\,n_s) \]
📖 Plain-language explanation for beginners
Four mechanical steps: build the matrices, diagonalise, search the best s, read the result.

Detailed notes

Theory and historical background

Chernoff (1952) proved that the detection error between two distributions decays as \(e^{-NC}\). For quantum states, Helstrom (1976) gave the optimal one-copy error, but \(N\) copies require the trace norm of a \(d^N\times d^N\) matrix. Ogawa and Hayashi (2004) conjectured the form of the exponent; Nussbaum and Szkoła proved the lower bound, Audenaert et al. the upper bound, and together they give \(\xi_{QCB}=-\ln\min_s\mathrm{Tr}\rho_0^s\rho_1^{1-s}\). Calsamiglia et al. (2008) apply it to qubits and Gaussian states.

Plain words, technique, table, calculation

Plain words: sending the same message several times makes guessing easier, and Q says how much each extra copy helps. Technique: \(Q=\min_s\sum_{ij}\lambda_i^s\mu_j^{1-s}|U_{ij}|^2\). Table: the tables of part 3. Calculation: see the examples below.

Example · Two coins (classical, quality testing, illustration)

Two coin presses give head probability \(p=0.3\) and \(q=0.6\) (assumption). Threshold \(\xi=0.4467\), \(C=0.04771\). After 100 tosses, \(\tfrac12e^{-100C}=0.00424\) while the exact \(P_e=9.84e-04\): the bound is 4.31 times larger but has the same exponent.

Example · Pure BPSK (optical communications)

\(\bar n=0.5\): \(Q=e^{-2}=0.1353\). One pulse: \(P_e=\tfrac12(1-\sqrt{1-Q})=0.03506\). Two pulses: \(\tfrac12(1-\sqrt{1-Q^2})=0.0046\), bound \(\tfrac12Q^2=0.009158\).

Example · Two qubits (quantum computing, illustration)

Two states stored in qubits with \(r=0.8\), tilted by \(90^\circ\). \(\lambda=0.9,0.1\); \(Q=\tfrac12(\sqrt{0.9}+\sqrt{0.1})^2=0.8\); \(\xi=0.2231\). We need \(N\ge\ln500/0.2231\), that is \(28\) copies for an error below \(10^{-3}\).

Example · Thermal noise (microwave links, illustration)

\(\alpha=0.7\), \(n_{th}=0.1\): \(\beta=\ln11=2.398\), \(\tanh(\beta/4)=0.5367\), \(Q=\exp(-1.96\cdot0.5367)=0.3493\). Without noise \(Q=e^{-1.96}=0.1409\).

Example · FSO fading (meteorology, illustration)

\(\sigma_R^2=1\), \(\bar n=0.5\): \(e^{-2}=0.1353\); \(\mathbb E[e^{-2I}]=0.2717\); \(Q\) of the mixed states \(=0.3764\). From \(Q=0.5164\) we get \(\xi=0.661\), the Helstrom error \(0.122\) lies between \(0.07181\) and \(0.2582\).

Case study and exam traps

Case: Calsamiglia et al. compare \(D_{QC}\), \(D_{CC}\) and the fidelity bounds in Fig. 2 (p. 12) and find identical per-copy measurements fall short of collective ones. Traps: (1) forgetting the factor \(\tfrac12\) in \(\tfrac12Q^N\); (2) mixing \(|\alpha|\) with \(|\alpha|^2\) in \(e^{-4|\alpha|^2}\); (3) the fidelity convention; (4) averaging \(Q\) instead of averaging the states.
