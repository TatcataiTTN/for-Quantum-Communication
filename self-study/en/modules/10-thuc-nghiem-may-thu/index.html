Standard quantum limit and the Helstrom bound

Standard quantum limit and Helstrom bound (binary)
\[ P_e^{\rm hom}=\tfrac12\,\mathrm{erfc}\!\bigl(\sqrt{2\bar n}\bigr),\qquad P_e^{\rm Hel}=\tfrac12\Bigl(1-\sqrt{1-e^{-4\bar n}}\Bigr) \]
  • \(\bar n=|\alpha|^2\)mean photon number of each state
  • \(|\pm\alpha\rangle\)the two BPSK coherent states
  • \(\mathrm{erfc}\)complementary error function
\(\bar n\)HomodyneHelstromRatio

Homodyne is the optimal Gaussian measurement, so it serves as the "standard quantum limit"; the gap to \(P_e^{\rm Hel}\) is what practical receivers try to close.

📖 Plain-language explanation for beginners
An ordinary homodyne measurement errs more than quantum theory allows. The receivers in this module are ways to build real devices that approach that level.

Kennedy: displace to vacuum, then count photons

Kennedy receiver
\[ \hat\Pi_{\rm no\,click}=\hat D(\alpha)|0\rangle\langle0|\hat D^\dagger(\alpha)=|\alpha\rangle\langle\alpha|,\qquad P_e^{\rm Ken}=\tfrac12e^{-4\bar n} \]
  • \(\hat D(\alpha)\)displacement operator (beam splitter + local field)
  • \(e^{-4\bar n}\)chance that |−α> shifted to |−2α> still gives no click: |<0|2α>|²

Displace \(|\alpha\rangle\) to the vacuum. If the input is \(|\alpha\rangle\) there is never a click; if it is \(|{-}\alpha\rangle\) the state becomes \(|{-}2\alpha\rangle\) and clicks with probability \(1-e^{-4|\alpha|^2}\). An error occurs only for \(|{-}\alpha\rangle\) with no click.

Numbers: \(\bar n=1\Rightarrow P_e^{\rm Ken}=0.00916\) versus homodyne \(0.02275\).

📖 Plain-language explanation for beginners
Idea: tune the detector so that one hypothesis gives perfect darkness. Seeing light then proves it is not that hypothesis.

Three measurements in phase space

The click detector \(\{\hat\Pi_{\rm click},\hat\Pi_{\rm no\,click}\}\) splits phase space in two halves; the displacement only decides which state is sent to \(|0\rangle\). General rule: \(P_e=\sum_{m\neq l}P(H_m|H_l)P(H_l)\).

📖 Plain-language explanation for beginners
Left: homodyne projects on one axis. Middle: Kennedy moves one state to the origin. Right: optimal displacement overshoots slightly to cut errors.

Error exponent: heterodyne is four times slower than Helstrom

Error exponent and the factor of four
\[ \kappa=\min_{m\neq m^{\prime}}\frac{\Vert \boldsymbol\alpha_m-\boldsymbol\alpha_{m^{\prime}}\Vert ^2}{N},\qquad \xi^{\rm Hel}=\kappa,\qquad \xi^{\rm Het}=\tfrac{\kappa}{4} \]
  • \(\xi=-\lim_{N\to\infty}\tfrac1N\ln P_E\)error exponent of a receiver
  • \(N\)mean energy (photons) of the ensemble

For QPSK neighbouring states satisfy \(\Vert \alpha_m-\alpha_{m^{\prime}}\Vert ^2=2N\), so \(\kappa=2\). Numerical check: the slope \(-\mathrm d\ln P_e/\mathrm dN\) of Helstrom between \(N=14\) and \(16\) is \(1.99\), for heterodyne \(0.53\), close to \(\kappa/4=0.5\).

(Nair, Guha, Tan, arXiv:1212.2048, Eq. (12), (15), pp. 3 and 5)

📖 Plain-language explanation for beginners
For strong signals every extra photon cuts the error exponentially. Heterodyne uses only a quarter of the maximum decay rate.

The QPSK constellation used in the experiments

\(|\alpha_n\rangle\) with \(\alpha_n=|\alpha|e^{i(n-\frac12)\pi/2}\), \(p_n=\tfrac14\). For \(|\alpha|^2\gg1\): \(P_e^{\rm Hel}\approx\tfrac12e^{-2|\alpha|^2}\) (Müller 2012, p. 4).

Numerical ratio: \(P_e^{\rm Hel}/(\tfrac12e^{-2\bar n})=\)1.0004 at \(\bar n=4\).

📖 Plain-language explanation for beginners
QPSK carries two bits per pulse with four phases 90 degrees apart. It is the main problem in the first two experiments.

The hybrid homodyne and photon-counting scheme

The beam is split by \(T=t^2\), \(R=r^2=1-t^2\). Homodyne (reflected part) removes half the plane; the result steers \(\hat D(\gamma)\) before the click detector (transmitted part).

\(P_e^{\rm HD\text{-}K}=1-\sum_m p_m P_{\rm succ}^{{\rm HD},m}P_{\rm succ}^{{\rm K},m}\),\ \(p_m=\tfrac14\).

📖 Plain-language explanation for beginners
Two steps in series: a coarse continuous measurement drops half of the options, then photon counting settles the remaining two.

The homodyne stage selects a half plane

Homodyne stage
\[ \hat\Pi_+^{\rm HD}=\int_0^\infty\!\mathrm dp\,|p\rangle\langle p|,\qquad P_{\rm err}^{\rm HD}=\tfrac12\Bigl(1-\mathrm{erf}\bigl[\sqrt2\,\tfrac{r|\alpha|}{\sqrt2}\bigr]\Bigr)=\tfrac12\bigl(1-\mathrm{erf}(r|\alpha|)\bigr) \]
  • \(p\)measured P quadrature
  • \(r\)reflectivity amplitude of the beam splitter
  • \(1/\sqrt2\)effective amplitude reduced by projecting on one axis

The optimal displacement for QPSK

Optimised displacement (OD)
\[ \frac{t}{\sqrt2}\alpha=\beta\tanh\bigl(\sqrt2\,t\alpha\beta\bigr),\qquad \gamma=\beta+i\,\frac{|t\alpha|}{\sqrt2} \]
  • \(\beta\)displacement along X
  • \(\gamma\)total displacement
📖 Plain-language explanation for beginners
Instead of moving one state exactly to zero, we overshoot a little so both states are about equally far from the origin, reducing errors for weak signals.

Optimal displacement parameters versus power

\(|\gamma|=\sqrt{|t\alpha|^2/2+|\beta|^2}\), \(\varphi=\arctan\dfrac{|t\alpha|}{\sqrt2|\beta|}\). Weak-signal limit: \(|\gamma|^2\to0.5\) , \(\varphi\to0\). Bright signal: \(\varphi\to\pi/4\).

The elimination receiver for four coherent states

\(\hat\Pi_i^{\rm USD}=:\hat R_i:-\sum_j\hat S_{i,j}+\sum_{j,k}\hat T_{i,j,k}-:\hat R_1\hat R_2\hat R_3\hat R_4:\), \(\hat\Pi_?=1-\sum_i\hat\Pi_i\).

A click in port \(i\) eliminates hypothesis \(i\); only when exactly one port stays dark is the conclusion certain (Becerra 2013, Eq. (1), p. 2).

📖 Plain-language explanation for beginners
Four branches, each ruling out one phase. If three click and one stays dark, the remaining phase is certain; other patterns return "inconclusive".

The sequential waveform nulling receiver

Split the signal into \(L\) slices (\(L\ge M-2\)); displace each by \(-\alpha_\mu/\sqrt L\) and count; on a click \(\mu\leftarrow\mu+1\). Decide \(\hat m=\mu\).

SWN error bound and exponent
\[ \begin{aligned}P_E^{\rm SWN}&\le\sum_{m=1}^M\pi_m\sum_{K=0}^{m-2}\binom LK e^{-\kappa(1-K/L)N}\\ \xi^{\rm SWN}&\ge\Bigl(1-\tfrac{M-2}{L}\Bigr)\kappa\end{aligned} \]
📖 Plain-language explanation for beginners
Like Kennedy but testing each hypothesis in turn on successive slices of the signal. More slices means closer to the quantum limit.

A sign trap in the OD error formula

Trap: the sign of β² in Eq. (14)

The text extracted from the PDF prints \(\exp(-t^2|\alpha|^2/2+|\beta|^2)\). A Fock-space check with \(a=0.8\): \(P_e=\tfrac12(1-|\langle\beta|a\rangle|^2)+\tfrac12|\langle\beta|{-}a\rangle|^2=0.032755\).

Form confirmed numerically
\[ P_e^{\rm OD}=\tfrac12-e^{-a^2-\beta^2}\sinh(2a\beta),\quad a=\tfrac{t|\alpha|}{\sqrt2} \]
  • \(-a^2-\beta^2\)gives 0.032755, matches Fock
  • \(-a^2+\beta^2\)gives a negative value, impossible

When implementing a formula, always check it against a direct simulation.

📖 Plain-language explanation for beginners
One wrong sign gives a negative probability. A small Fock test catches it immediately.

Limits of the hybrid experiment

Trap: a proof-of-principle experiment

Müller et al. state that the feed-forward is emulated in post-processing (only data whose displacement matched the homodyne result are kept), the analysis assumes unit efficiencies, and losses are folded into an effective splitting ratio. The data therefore show the concept, not a real-time receiver.

Effective splitting ratio
\[ T\to T^{\prime}=\frac{\eta_{\rm APD}T}{\eta_{\rm APD}T+\eta_{HD}R},\qquad R\to R^{\prime}=\frac{\eta_{HD}R}{\eta_{\rm APD}T+\eta_{HD}R} \]
  • \(\eta_{\rm APD}=0.63\)APD efficiency
  • \(\eta_{HD}=0.83\)homodyne efficiency

Check: \(T=0.53\Rightarrow T^{\prime}=0.461\).

📖 Plain-language explanation for beginners
The result is sound in principle but needs a real-time version with real loss figures before applications.

Extension: sequential testing reaches the Chernoff exponent

The sequential testing (ST) receiver needs only LOCC
\[ P_E^{\rm ST}\le\sum_{m=1}^M\pi_m\sum_{K=0}^{m-2}\binom nK F_{\max}^{\,n-K},\qquad F_{\max}=\max_{m\neq m^{\prime}}|\langle\psi_m|\psi_{m^{\prime}}\rangle|^2,\qquad \xi^{\rm ST}=\xi_{\rm QC} \]
  • \(\Pi_m=|\psi_m\rangle\langle\psi_m|\)binary projection on each copy
  • \(\xi_{\rm QC}=\min_{m\neq m^{\prime}}-\ln|\langle\psi_m|\psi_{m^{\prime}}\rangle|^2\)quantum Chernoff exponent for pure states

For two states \(|\pm\alpha\rangle\): \(F_{\max}=e^{-4|\alpha|^2}\), \(\xi_{\rm QC}=4|\alpha|^2\). With \(n\) copies: \(P_E\lesssim e^{-4n|\alpha|^2}\) in exponent (Nair 2013, Eqs. (7), (17) to (19), pp. 3 and 7).

📖 Plain-language explanation for beginners
With many copies of the same state, measuring one copy at a time and passing the result on still reaches the best possible exponent.

The common thread
\[ \xi^{\rm Het}=\tfrac\kappa4\ \le\ \xi^{\rm SWN}\ \le\ \xi^{\rm Hel}=\kappa \]
📖 Plain-language explanation for beginners
All four share the idea: displace to null one hypothesis and count photons. They differ in the number of states and the feedback.

Summary and open points

Takeaways

  • Kennedy beats homodyne when \(\bar n>0.38\)
  • Optimised displacement helps at every \(\bar n\), \(|\gamma|^2\ge0.5\)
  • Heterodyne is four times slower than Helstrom: \(\xi^{\rm Het}=\kappa/4\)
  • USD wins at small n̄ when compared at equal p_C

Open points

  • A real-time QPSK receiver is not reported in the three papers read
  • These papers contain no analysis over turbulent channels
  • Part 3 numbers are recomputed, not measured
Key formulas
\[ P_e^{\rm Ken}=\tfrac12e^{-4\bar n},\quad P_e^{\rm Hel}=\tfrac12\bigl(1-\sqrt{1-e^{-4\bar n}}\bigr),\quad \xi^{\rm SWN}\ge\bigl(1-\tfrac{M-2}L\bigr)\kappa \]
📖 Plain-language explanation for beginners
If you remember one thing: practical receivers displace and then count photons, and the remaining gap to Helstrom is an open hardware problem.

Exam traps
  • Confusing |α| with |α|²: n̄=|α|².
  • Forgetting the ½ before erfc or exp.
  • Comparing raw USD p_e with heterodyne.
  • Using Eq. (14) with +β².

Background: Kennedy and Dolinar (1973) laid the base, Helstrom (1976) gave the bound. Cook 2007 and Wittmann 2008 realised the binary case; Becerra, Müller and Nair extended to M states.
